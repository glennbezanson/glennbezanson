# Atlanta's AI-security calendar hides in four circuits

Metro Atlanta has well over 150 distinct AI, AI-security, governance and enterprise-AI events and groups in the notes, not five, and the reason the same handful keeps surfacing is structural: the good rooms sit in four circuits that do not cross-promote (practitioner/community, TAG and the professional chapters, invite-only executive, and vendor/channel field marketing), plus a university seminar layer and a Luma-only dinner layer that never reach conference calendars. The most urgent fact is the week of **November 16-19, 2026**: the TAG AI Summit, the NACD Atlanta "AI Governance Circuit", and the Gartner/Evanta CDAO summit all fall on **Tuesday November 17**, and Thursday November 19 stacks the GeorgiaCIO ORBIE breakfast, the Evanta CISO summit, a Georgia Tech security talk, OWASP Atlanta and an in-person AICamp night. Three money-and-speaking deadlines follow immediately: the **FabCon 2027 CFP closes November 22**, **Optimized AI Conference early pricing ends November 27** (with a free "Decision Makers" pass for senior managers and above), and the OAI CFP runs to February 28, 2027. For 2027 only about three dozen dates are organizer-confirmed, and nine of those are Gartner/Evanta programs a solution-provider employee can realistically enter only through sponsorship; almost everything else is a cadence estimate that needs a re-check in January-February 2027. The best small rooms for this reader are OWASP Atlanta, ISSA Metro Atlanta, BSides Atlanta (about 20 AI talks in 2026 for $50-60), the four Microsoft user groups at the Improving office in Alpharetta, AI Tinkerers, the Georgia Tech SCP Security Talks, GTRI's ASCCENT AI Security Summit (no 2026 edition found; email the organizers), and law-firm and advisory-firm sessions gated by relationship rather than buyer status. No standing Atlanta group dedicated to AI security or AI governance was found, which the notes treat as a real gap and an opening. This inventory is not exhaustive: all six research passes ran out of web-search budget, and the final section lists every area that was not checked, none of which should be read as "nothing exists".

**How to read the labels.** The notes' evidence labels are preserved throughout. **C** = date or fact on an organizer-controlled page the researcher opened. **S** = search-result summary of an organizer page that was not opened. **A** = aggregator or third-party listing only, unverified. **E** = estimated from past cadence, not announced. Access codes: **Open** (anyone can register, free or paid), **Member** (individual membership needed), **Invite** (invitation, vetting or request-to-join), **Sponsor** (vendor companies must sponsor to be in the room), **Public-sector only**. Quality verdicts are the researchers' judgments from format, organizer type, speaker rosters and RSVP counts; no independent attendee reviews were collected for any event.

## Conclusion

The practical shift is from hunting for conferences to holding standing positions in four distribution channels. Two or three personal memberships (ISACA or IIA, IAPP, TAG), a Luma follow on the AI Collective calendar, the Georgia Tech feeds and a dozen named hosts would have surfaced nearly every small room in this inventory, and direct asks to account managers cover the vendor layer that no calendar reaches. The sponsor-gated executive circuit is better treated as a budgeting decision than a discovery problem: ORBIE's Partner program and a single Evanta boardroom are the only sanctioned ways into those rooms, and the notes found no evidence a governance lead at a solutions provider gets in otherwise.

The more interesting finding is the vacancy. Atlanta has BSides talks on MCP security and ISO 42001, a GTRI summit on AI security for critical infrastructure with no announced successor, CISO roundtables on shadow AI, and board luncheons on AI governance, yet no standing group owns AI security or AI governance, and only one event found, a vendor dinner, put AI gateways on its agenda. Open CFPs at OAI (to February 28) and DevOpsDays Atlanta, monthly speaker slots at ISSA, OWASP and Azure in the ATL, and the estimated spring CFPs for Atlanta Cloud+AI and BSides give a Head of AI & Governance a realistic path to being the person who convenes that room rather than the one who hears about it afterwards. Three checks should happen this week because they change the plan: the true dates of Atlanta AI Week 2027 and Devnexus 2027, and whether ASCCENT will run again.
